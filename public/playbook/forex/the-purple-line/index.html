Setup: The Purple Line — Rank SSS

Concept#

The purple line is a level placed on the chart by the trader at an intuitively important area on the TPO chart. Not by rule. Not by formula. By internalized criteria built from screen time, pattern recognition, and a deep read of market structure. These lines are placed before price arrives — never after.

When price interacts with a purple line in a meaningful way, that interaction is the trade.

Direction is not known in advance. Whether you will be long or short depends entirely on how price behaves when it gets there. The level is the reference. The reaction is the signal. This trade shares DNA with the Intuitive Strike — the same art-over-science nature — but here there is an anchor on the chart before you ever enter.

This is the closest thing to pure art in the playbook. Beyond nuance. And when right, beyond profitable.

Jumping the gun is donating money to the market. It already has enough.

Criteria#

Price interacts with a purple line on the chart.

That is the criteria. Everything else is reading the interaction — which cannot be reduced to a checklist. The line was placed at a level that matters. When price arrives, you watch. You feel the reaction. You read the context. Then and only then do you act.

Ideally, you wait for a new profile to begin before trading the interaction. A touch at 11pm CST means nothing without context — wait for the profile change and observe how the market treats the level in the new session. That fresh open, that first response to the level under new conditions, is where the trade lives.

More interaction with a line over time may strengthen it. Or it may weaken it. Read each touch in context and trust your read. There is no universal answer.

Entry#

  • Trigger: Meaningful interaction with the purple line, ideally at or after a profile boundary
  • Direction: Determined by the reaction — not decided before price arrives
  • Stop: Defined by the interaction. A line that has been clearly accepted through is an invalidated trade. Exit.
  • Target: The next purple line if one exists. These levels act as magnets to each other.
  • Exit: Scale as the move develops. Leave a runner if the context supports it. Reassess at the next purple line.

Rules#

  1. The line must have been placed before price arrived. A level drawn after the fact is observation, not a setup.
  2. Wait for the profile to change before acting on an interaction. Let the new session confirm the reaction.
  3. Do not decide direction in advance. Let the market speak first.
  4. Do not fall in love with a test. If the interaction produces nothing — no story, no reaction — walk away. The line may matter again later. Or it may not.
  5. Each pair has a personality. A purple line trade in the pound will not look like one in the aussie. Do not carry one pair’s behavior into another. Stay present to the instrument you are in.
  6. Size appropriately. These trades, when right, are among the most profitable in the playbook. Size up when conviction is there. Size down when it isn’t.
  7. Find balance. Trust your instincts, and remain objective on the odds at the same time. Those two things are not in conflict — they are both required.
  8. Do not beat yourself up when you miss one or get stopped out. Be patient. Stay in rhythm. The lines will still be there.

> When You Feel It — Take The Trade#

I cannot understate this enough.

If you felt that feeling — that internal certainty — and you took the trade, and it doesn’t work out: that is okay. Long as it was grounded. You did your job. Move on.

But if you felt that feeling, and you did not pull the trigger — even if you could have adjusted size to manage the risk — and the trade bangs: that is unacceptable. That is the worse evil. A loss grounded in conviction is recoverable. A missed trade you knew is a different kind of damage.

Be well slept. Non-negotiable. Be well fed. Non-negotiable.

You need to be in the right headspace to hear what the market is telling you. If you are not, you will second-guess the signal. Do not put yourself in a position where your body is working against your edge.

When the feeling is there and the context is grounded — take the trade.

Notes#

This trade cannot be fully taught. It can only be developed. The criteria live inside the trader, built over time through repetition and honest review. The purple line is the externalization of that internal knowledge — a mark on the chart that says this level matters, placed before the market proves it.

The trap is impatience. The line pulls your attention and the temptation to act early is real. Resist it. Wait for the profile. Wait for the reaction. The market is under no obligation to be obvious.

When the trade is right, it will feel different from a forced read. Trust that. Be patient until you feel it.

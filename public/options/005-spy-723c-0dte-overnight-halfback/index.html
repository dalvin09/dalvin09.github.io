Rationale#

Going into the session, the prior day’s price action suggested the market couldn’t support further upside expansion — value had left the initial balance and formed a 45-degree line. Leading into the open however, sudden momentum pushed both ES and NQ into slight gap ups with the overnight halfback sitting below. Both markets opened and moved lower to test that level. ES came in for a full test. NQ did not fully test and began turning higher before reaching it. Once NQ broke the overnight high, the odds shifted strongly toward breakaway value and continued upside — that was the trigger.

Execution#

Entered the 723 call at $0.65 on the NQ overnight high break confirmation. A period was extended and B period began one time framing higher — the thesis was playing out cleanly. With the position already up 203% and representing 10% of total account size, closing the full contract on a Friday was the right risk management call. Sold at $1.97. Ideally would have closed 70% and left a runner into the new all-time high, but with one contract that wasn’t possible.

Mistake / Lesson#

Capital constraints continue to limit proper scaling. With only one contract available, there’s no ability to take partial profits and leave runners — it’s all or nothing. The runner into the all-time high was left on the table. As the account grows, the SSS-ranked overnight halfback setup justifies heavier sizing and a scaled exit structure.

Review#

What went well: Read the overnight halfback setup correctly, identified NQ’s failure to fully test as early strength, and used the overnight high break as the entry trigger. Held through A period extension and B period one time framing without shaking out.
What to improve: Need to grow the account to properly scale into SSS-ranked setups — one contract forces a binary exit when the setup deserves a runner.
Grade: A

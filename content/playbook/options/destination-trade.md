## Concept

The **Destination Trade** is a range fade built on correlated failure. When both ES and NQ test the overnight high or overnight low and neither can achieve acceptance beyond it, the market has revealed its hand — the overnight extreme is defended, and the other end of range is now the destination.

This is not a scalp. It is a full range trade, and the target is the opposite overnight extreme.

> Context is not secondary to the setup — it is equal to it. The same technical picture in the wrong environment produces a completely different outcome.

---

## Criteria (All must be met)

- [ ] **Both ES and NQ** test the same overnight extreme — high or low
- [ ] Neither instrument achieves acceptance beyond the overnight extreme
- [ ] The failure holds — price stalls, rejects, and begins to move away from the extreme
- [ ] Market is in a **bracketed or sideways structure** — this is not a trending market trade
- [ ] **VIX is below 20** — elevated volatility degrades the range structure this trade depends on
- [ ] No major macro catalyst that could override range behavior (FOMC, CPI, earnings)
- [ ] The longer the initial failure holds, the higher the conviction

---

## Entry

- **Trigger:** Confirmed failure at the overnight extreme with both ES and NQ rejecting the level — no acceptance
- **Entry:** Fade the failed extreme — short if overnight high fails, long if overnight low fails
- **Stop:** Acceptance through the overnight extreme by either instrument
- **Target:** The opposite overnight extreme — full range
- **Sizing:** Appropriate to account size — this trade can be volatile. Do not oversize into the setup, especially early in the rejection.

---

## The Setup in Context

**Bearish scenario:** Both ES and NQ push into the overnight high, probe it, and fail. Neither can sustain trade above it. Price begins rotating back down. The overnight high is now resistance. The overnight low is the destination.

**Bullish scenario:** Both ES and NQ test down into the overnight low, fail to accept below it. Price lifts back. The overnight low is now support. The overnight high is the destination.

In both cases, the **correlated failure** of both instruments is the confirmation. One instrument failing alone is noise. Both failing together is signal.

---

## Weaknesses & When to Avoid

- **It looks implausible as it sets up** — this is the trade's defining weakness. The market appears to be breaking out, or breaking down, and the idea of fading it feels counterintuitive. This feeling is part of the trade. Trusting the setup when it looks wrong is a prerequisite for executing it correctly.
- **Exiting early** — because the setup looks implausible, the temptation to take a partial gain before reaching the destination is strong. Early exits consistently underperform relative to holding for the full range. Letting the trade work is not optional — it is the trade.
- **Trending markets** — this setup does not belong in a trending environment. In a strong directional tape, a failure at the overnight extreme is often a pause, not a reversal. The bracketed market context requirement is not a suggestion.
- **VIX above 20** — elevated volatility breaks the range structure. Above 20, overnight extremes become less reliable as containment levels and the range expands unpredictably.
- **Single instrument failure** — if only ES or only NQ rejects the level, the setup is not confirmed. Correlated failure is the signal; isolated failure is not.
- **Macro events** — FOMC, CPI, major earnings, and similar catalysts can override range structure entirely. Avoid on high-impact event days.

---

## The Patience Problem

This trade is particularly difficult to hold because the failure often looks temporary in real time. The market will probe the overnight extreme, appear to break through, pull back, test again — and only then truly fail. The process of failure is not clean. It is messy, extended, and designed to shake out early entries.

**The rule:** The longer the initial failure holds at the extreme, the greater the probability of reaching the other end of range. Time spent rejecting the level is not a warning sign — it is confirmation building.

## Notes

This trade lives and dies on context. A textbook rejection at the overnight extreme in a trending, high-VIX market is not this trade. The bracketed market structure is the environment that gives the destination its credibility — in a range, the other end of range is always there. The trade is simply identifying when the market has confirmed it cannot get out of that range on one side, and positioning for the return trip to the other.

The implausibility of the setup as it forms is not a bug. It is why the trade works.
